## What Should a Cleaning Company CRM Actually Do?

Before comparing tools, it helps to define what "CRM" means in the cleaning industry context. A CRM (Customer Relationship Management platform) for a cleaning company should, at minimum:

- **Capture leads** from your website, ads, or Google Business Profile
- **Automate follow-up** via SMS and email so no lead goes cold
- **Track your pipeline** from quote request to signed recurring client
- **Manage customer history** — what properties they have, frequency, special notes
- **Collect and publish reviews** to keep your Google rating strong
- **Send reminders and confirmations** to reduce no-shows

Where cleaning-specific tools like Jobber and Housecall Pro go further is in **scheduling crews, dispatching, and invoicing** — which a general CRM like GoHighLevel doesn't cover natively. That's the core trade-off you need to understand before choosing.

## Where Jobber and Housecall Pro Win

I want to be honest here: GoHighLevel does not replace a field service management tool. If your daily workflow involves:

- Dispatching 3+ crews to different addresses
- Tracking job completion status in real time
- Generating invoices on-site
- Managing recurring job schedules across dozens of clients

...then you need Jobber, Housecall Pro, or ServiceTitan alongside your CRM. These tools are built for field service and they do it better than GoHighLevel will likely ever need to.

**Jobber** is the cleanest option for smaller cleaning businesses ($69–$249/month depending on features). It handles quoting, scheduling, client management, and payments beautifully. The weakness is marketing automation — it's basic at best.

**Housecall Pro** adds more marketing features than Jobber, including automated review requests and a basic email marketing tool. For a mid-size cleaning company that wants one tool, it's a solid choice. But it still doesn't match GoHighLevel's depth on the sales automation side.

**ServiceTitan** is enterprise-level and priced accordingly — I wouldn't recommend it for a cleaning company doing under $1M/year in revenue. The onboarding alone can cost thousands.
