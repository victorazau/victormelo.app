GoHighLevel and Keap (formerly Infusionsoft) are both CRM and marketing automation platforms, but they serve very different buyers in 2026. GoHighLevel is built for agencies and growth-focused businesses that want an all-in-one stack under one roof; Keap is a more traditional small-business CRM with strong automation roots but a narrower toolset. If you're trying to decide between the two, this comparison will give you a clear, honest answer based on real implementation experience.

## Key Takeaways

- GoHighLevel starts at $97/mo and covers CRM, funnels, email, SMS, booking, and more — Keap starts at $299/mo for two users with a thinner feature set.
- Keap has a longer track record and a mature contact-management UI that some small business owners find easier to start with.
- GoHighLevel's white-label and multi-location capabilities make it the clear winner for agencies; Keap has no equivalent.
- I've implemented both for clients — GoHighLevel almost always wins on total cost of ownership once you account for the tools it replaces.
- Neither platform is perfect: Keap's ecosystem is simpler but pricier for what you get; GoHighLevel has a steeper learning curve but far higher ceiling.

## What Is Keap (and What Was Infusionsoft)?

Keap is the brand name that replaced Infusionsoft back in 2019. The company kept the Infusionsoft product alive for legacy customers under the name "Keap Max Classic," while launching simpler tiers (Keap Pro, Keap Max) for new users. In 2026, the platform is consolidating under the Keap brand, but many people still search for "Infusionsoft" because of its decade-long reputation in the small business CRM space.

At its core, Keap is a contact management and marketing automation tool. It handles email campaigns, basic SMS, quotes and invoices, appointment booking, and pipeline management. It was genuinely innovative when it launched — drag-and-drop campaign builder, tagging, behavior-based automation — but the market has caught up and, in many areas, passed it.

GoHighLevel, by contrast, launched in 2018 specifically for digital marketing agencies. It was designed from day one to let one team manage dozens of client accounts inside a single platform, with white-labeling baked in. That origin shapes every decision the product makes.

## Where Keap Actually Wins

I want to be fair here because honest comparisons are the only comparisons worth reading.

**1. Contact management depth.** Keap's tagging system and contact record UI are genuinely mature. If you have a large contact database with complex segmentation needs and your team lives inside the CRM all day, Keap's interface can feel more natural, especially for users who aren't technical.

**2. Invoicing and payment workflows.** Keap has a solid quote-to-invoice-to-payment flow that some service businesses (consultants, contractors) love. It's tighter than what GoHighLevel offers out of the box, though GoHighLevel's payments have improved significantly.

**3. Ecosystem familiarity.** If your team has been using Keap for years and has hundreds of automations built out, switching has a real cost. I never recommend switching platforms just to switch — only when the new platform genuinely solves a problem the current one can't.

**4. Customer support reputation.** Keap's support has historically been solid for its price bracket. GoHighLevel's support quality can vary — their community and documentation are excellent, but live support response times have been inconsistent in my experience.

## Where GoHighLevel Wins (And It's Most Categories)

**Agency use cases.** There is simply no comparison here. If you manage marketing for clients — even just two or three — GoHighLevel's sub-account structure lets you run everything in one dashboard. Keap doesn't have this. Period.

**All-in-one value.** Funnels, websites, booking, courses, reputation management, call tracking, two-way SMS, email, social planner — GoHighLevel includes all of this. With Keap, each of these is either missing or requires a paid integration.

**AI and automation ceiling.** GoHighLevel's AI features (conversation AI, content AI, review response AI) are growing fast. Keap's AI development is comparatively slow. If you're building automations that need to scale with intelligence, GoHighLevel is ahead.

**White-labeling.** On the Unlimited plan at $297/mo, you can white-label GoHighLevel and present it to clients as your own software. This is how agencies build recurring revenue on top of GHL — something I covered in depth in my post on [how agencies make money with GoHighLevel SaaS mode](/blog/how-agencies-make-money-gohighlevel-saas-mode).

**Price-to-feature ratio.** At $97/mo vs $299/mo for fewer features, GoHighLevel wins this decisively.

## Who Should Choose Keap?

Keap makes sense if:
- You're a solo consultant or small service business with a very simple funnel (lead → quote → invoice → follow-up)
- You've already invested heavily in Keap automations and your team knows it well
- You don't have clients to manage — just your own business
- You prioritize a polished invoice and payment workflow over marketing breadth
- You have no interest in building funnels, websites, or running SMS campaigns

Even in these cases, I'd still recommend evaluating GoHighLevel seriously before committing to Keap's pricing, because the Starter plan at $97/mo covers most of what Keap charges $299/mo for.

## Who Should Choose GoHighLevel?

GoHighLevel is the right call if:
- You run a marketing agency or manage multiple client accounts
- You want to consolidate 5–10 tools into one monthly subscription
- You're building (or want to build) an online presence with funnels, booking, and email sequences
- You want to resell the platform as your own software (SaaS mode)
- You need SMS + email + voice + social in one place
- You're a coach, consultant, or course creator who also wants marketing automation

In my experience implementing platforms for agencies and service businesses, GoHighLevel fits probably 80% of the buyer profiles I encounter. The 20% who might still lean toward Keap are usually legacy users or very simple solo operations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q:** Is GoHighLevel better than Keap for small businesses?
**A:** For most small businesses, yes. GoHighLevel's Starter plan at $97/mo includes features that Keap charges $299/mo or more for, plus adds funnel building, website hosting, and SMS that Keap doesn't cover natively. Unless you're deeply embedded in Keap's ecosystem already, GoHighLevel offers more value.

**Q:** Can GoHighLevel replace Keap/Infusionsoft completely?
**A:** In most cases, yes. GoHighLevel covers CRM, pipelines, email automation, SMS, booking, and payment collection — all core Keap functions. The main gap is Keap's mature invoicing workflow, though GoHighLevel's payments and invoicing have improved considerably in 2025–2026.

**Q:** How much does Keap cost per month in 2026?
**A:** Keap Pro starts around $299/mo for 2 users; Keap Max is approximately $399/mo for 3 users. Additional users are billed on top. Contact limits also affect pricing. GoHighLevel's Starter is $97/mo with no per-user fees.

**Q:** Does GoHighLevel have a free trial?
**A:** Yes. GoHighLevel offers a 14-day free trial on all plans. Keap also offers a trial period, though the terms have varied. The 14-day GoHighLevel trial gives you full access to test the platform before committing.

**Q:** What is the difference between Keap and Infusionsoft?
**A:** Infusionsoft rebranded to Keap in 2019. The legacy Infusionsoft product (now called Keap Max Classic) is still available for existing customers. New users sign up under the Keap brand. Functionally, the platforms share the same underlying technology but Keap's newer tiers have a simplified interface compared to the original Infusionsoft.

**Q:** Is GoHighLevel hard to learn compared to Keap?
**A:** GoHighLevel has a steeper initial learning curve because it does far more. Keap's simplified tiers have a cleaner onboarding experience for beginners. That said, GoHighLevel's community, documentation, and onboarding resources have improved significantly — most people get functional within a week of focused setup.

**Q:** Can I migrate from Keap to GoHighLevel?
**A:** Yes. GoHighLevel supports contact and data imports via CSV, and many agencies offer done-for-you migration services. The automation logic will need to be rebuilt in GHL's campaign builder, which takes time but is very doable. I'd budget 2–4 weeks for a full migration depending on complexity.

## Conclusion

After running both platforms across real client implementations, the verdict is clear for most readers landing on this page: GoHighLevel delivers a significantly broader feature set at a lower price point. Keap had a head start and built a loyal user base, but in 2026 it's hard to justify $299/mo for a platform that still requires third-party tools for funnels, websites, and proper SMS campaigns.

Keap earns its place for legacy users and very simple solo operations where the invoicing workflow genuinely shines. But for agencies, growing service businesses, and anyone consolidating their tech stack, GoHighLevel is the smarter investment.

If you're on the fence, the best move is to test GoHighLevel yourself during the 14-day free trial — set up one pipeline, one automation, and one booking calendar. That's usually all it takes to see whether it fits your workflow.
